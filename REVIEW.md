# REVIEW.md
During the review of the starter `app/main.py`, I focused on timestamp validation, attendance punch-in and punch-out handling, attendance corrections, analytics calculations, database indexing, and query-plan inspection. Timestamp validation was improved to require epoch milliseconds, and invalid timestamp inputs were tested to ensure they return HTTP 422. The punch-out logic was checked and updated to validate open attendance records, prevent duplicate punch-outs, reject invalid time intervals, and calculate work hours, overtime, and half-day status. The attendance listing endpoint was checked to ensure timestamps are returned in the expected format and MongoDB `_id` values are excluded from responses. The attendance correction endpoint was reviewed for input validation, correction history, and no-op corrections.

The analytics endpoints were also reviewed. Monthly statistics were checked against the requested month, and the department summary aggregation was adjusted to include eligible employees even when they have no attendance logs. Present-day calculations were updated to exclude weekends and count qualifying half-days as 0.5. The late leaderboard and department trend endpoints were checked for consistent ranking, date handling, and trend calculations. Database indexes were created for unique employee codes, unique employee/date attendance records, and date-based queries. Explain output was also checked to confirm index usage for supported queries.

Some behaviors were reviewed and considered expected rather than defects. For example, an empty monthly attendance result is valid when no records exist for that month, but department headcount should still include eligible employees. HTTP 422 is appropriate for invalid timestamps and no-op corrections, while HTTP 409 is appropriate for duplicate employee codes. An aggregation returning no matching attendance records is not itself a defect, provided employees without logs are handled correctly where required. These findings reflect the issues discussed and tested during development; each item should be checked against the original starter code before being described as an original defect.
